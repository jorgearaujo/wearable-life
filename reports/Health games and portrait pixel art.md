# Gentle health games endure; portrait scenes need verticals

The health-driven products that last for years are the ones that only add: Pikmin Bloom had a record fifth year, Pokémon Sleep's revenue fell only 3.2% in its third, and Walkr has run since 2014. The ones that died were killed by server costs, token economies or an owner's change of strategy, and no source found blames a design for being too gentle. No product found combines all of this project's constraints (several passive signals, a world instead of a score, nothing that can drop, nothing leaving the device); the closest, a 2026 iOS pet called Habitvs, has no world and shows a recovery score. That gap is partly explained by cost: loss aversion is the best-measured retention lever, and no controlled study shows a no-loss design retaining as well as a streak. Late data is the normal case: the two largest step games tell players to expect delays of "up to several hours", and Apple documents that the health store may be unreadable in the background while the phone is locked. Google Play names games as an approved Health Connect use; Apple has no such category, and its most documented rejection is for not making Health use visible in the interface, which pulls against a game that hides numbers. On composition, no shipped game was found that is portrait, pixel art, a side-view lake and cabin, and has live day/night and weather. The portrait scenes that work fill the height with vertical structure (a tall near tree, mirrored water, a ground plane seen from slightly above), and the classic wide recipe of sky, thin ground strip and reflection wastes a tall frame.

## How to read the sourcing

This report synthesises five sets of research notes dated 11 November 2026. It carries their verification marks through, because much of the evidence is thinner than it looks.

- **Unmarked claims** come from a page a researcher opened.
- **[snippet]** means the claim comes from a search-result summary of the linked page, which was not opened. Treat these as leads, not established facts.
- **"Verbatim per fetch"** applies to all Apple and Google policy quotes: they were extracted by a tool that summarises pages, and need re-checking on the live page before they are relied on in a store submission.
- **"Researcher's estimate" or "researcher's measurement"** applies to every band percentage, character size and art pixel size in Part 2. They were read off store screenshots or images by eye or by measuring pixel runs. No developer has stated any of them.
- **Inference** marks reasoning by the researchers or by this report, not a sourced fact.

Reddit could not be read directly, and Bulbapedia, the WalkScape wiki, GamesBeat, Medium, Tumblr and TIGSource blocked access. Player sentiment therefore comes from store reviews, press and forum summaries, and is thin on guilt and pressure specifically. No talks or videos were watched.

### Step-driven games: passive reads work, and the complaints are about silence

**Pikmin Bloom** is the closest large precedent for a calm world fed by steps. On iOS its steps are "linked to the Apple Health app", and the publisher tells players to expect "a delay of up to several hours" for the sync; the same page asks for Health access, location set to "Allow Always" and motion tracking, and warns that low-power modes can disable step sensors ([Scopely help centre](https://scopelyexplore.helpshift.com/hc/faq/2196-my-steps-aren-t-being-counted-correctly-ios/)). Reviewers describe seedlings that hatch after set step counts and a calendar-style "Lifelog" of the day [snippet] ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/reviews/mobile/pikmin-bloom)). Commercially it started slowly, with about 2 million downloads and roughly $473,000 of spending in its first two weeks [snippet] ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pikmin-bloom-two-million-downloads/)), then passed **$100 million lifetime on 1 December 2025** and earned a record $36.1 million in its fifth year [snippets] ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pikmin-bloom-hits-100m-after-four-years-as-2025-becomes-its-best-year-yet), [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pikmin-bloom-blossoms-in-fifth-year-with-record-36m/)). Launch-era complaints were about battery drain [snippet] ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/android-problems-uninstall-pikmin-bloom-17699737)). Whether flowers expire or anything decays with inactivity was not verified, and no retention or active-user figures were found. Reading the revenue curve as evidence of long retention is an inference.

**Pokémon GO's Adventure Sync** (announced 25 October 2018) is the reference implementation of reading distance from the health store with the app closed, counting it toward eggs and candy with weekly milestone rewards ([Pokémon GO news](https://pokemongo.com/en/news/adventure-sync)). It is also the reference for what goes wrong. The help page repeats the "up to several hours" delay, states that manually entered fitness data does not count, and says battery saver can disable the sensors ([Scopely help centre](https://scopelyexplore.helpshift.com/hc/faq/1325-troubleshooting-adventure-sync/)). In May 2023 a Reddit poll reported **87.6% of respondents had Adventure Sync problems**, and a widely shared post accused Niantic of "gaslighting the community by pretending adventure sync isn't broken" ([Charlie Intel](https://charlieintel.com/pokemon/pokemon-go-players-accuse-niantic-of-ignoring-adventure-sync-issues-251589)). The researchers' reading, which this report shares, is that the anger is about silent undercounting more than delay (inference). The official page still names only Apple Health and Google Fit; whether the game has moved to Health Connect was not verified.

**Walkr** (Fourdesire, published by Sparkful) turns steps into spaceship fuel for a planet-collecting idle game and was still receiving updates in November 2026, at 4.5 stars from about 8,100 US ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/walkr-gamified-fitness-walk/id834805518)). It reads only the iPhone's motion chip, and its help centre says "if steps are tracked by your Apple Watch, they won't show in Walkr" ([Sparkful help centre](https://sparkful.app/help/articles/im-wearing-my-apple-watch-when-i-run-so-why-dont-my-steps-show-in-walkr)). It is free with ads, a $1.99 ad remover, currency packs up to $99.99 and a $7.99 monthly subscription. A four-year player calls it "soft, calming, and sweet"; the worst reviews are about **progress lost when switching phones**, undercounting against a Fitbit, and running out of content ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/walkr-gamified-fitness-walk/id834805518)). Its Android data path, team size and any numbers were not found.

**WalkScape** (Not a Cult Oy, Finland) is the most relevant indie comparison: a pixel-art idle role-playing game where "you don't play actively, but instead gain progress by walking", in closed beta with access by invitation or by supporting the developers ([walkscape.app](https://walkscape.app/)). Its wiki says it reads steps only from Apple Health, Health Connect or the Samsung Health API [snippet] ([WalkScape wiki](https://wiki.walkscape.app/wiki/FAQs?oldid=5584)). A developer comment notes that Xiaomi's app connects to Health Connect but reports no steps to it, and a user on a firewalled network reported lost steps because steps are processed at login [snippets] ([r/WalkScape mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/WalkScape/comments/1hzmrnk/dear_devs_we_need_smartwatch_integration/m6sfv2r/?context=3), [r/WalkScape mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/WalkScape/comments/1f3hn6w/steps_not_being_counted_due_to_workplace_network)). A GamesRadar interview is headlined "Staying MTX and advertisement free is absolutely key", but only the headline was read ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/mmo/staying-mtx-and-advertisement-free-is-absolutely-key-almost-20-years-later-runescape-fan-turns-his-mmo-love-into-an-old-school-rpg-where-fitness-is-the-grind/)). Team size, pricing and an open-beta date were not found.

**Zombies, Run!** shows a durable niche and a fragile ownership story. It launched in 2012 as a $7.99 paid app, moved to free with a subscription in 2015, and by 2017 had 250,000 monthly players; co-founder Adrian Hon said of the content demand, "It's been like opening a gym" ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-slow-steady-progress-of-zombies-run/)). At its 2021 sale it had about 300,000 monthly users and roughly 50,000 paid subscribers [snippet] ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/olivex-has-acquired-fitness-game-developer-six-to-start-for-9-5-million)). The buyer laid off all but two staff in March 2025 [snippet] ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/finally-some-good-buyout-news-a-writer-just-bought-back-the-game-she-co-created-from-a-blockchain-company-so-it-doesnt-end-up-being-turned-into-ai-slop)), and co-creator Naomi Alderman bought the game back in November 2025 ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/novelist-naomi-alderman-reacquires-zombies-run-from-former-owner-olivex/)). How the app reads movement was not verified, and no source states whether it has streaks.

The guilt pattern appears in two places. Nintendo's 1998 **Pokémon Pikachu** pedometer gave one watt per twenty steps, and a neglected Pikachu "will express anger and eventually refuse to recognize the player" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Pikachu)); the 2009 Pokéwalker dropped that and tied steps to experience and new routes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9walker)). Google's **Fitbit Ace LTE** children's watch revives it: games stop and "prompt you to get moving in order to proceed", the eejie creature's happiness depends on daily goals, and an Engadget reviewer described its dejected look as a guilt-based motivator that worked on her while finding the creature shallow, "mostly feels like a mascot" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/wearables/fitbit-ace-lte-review-a-kids-activity-tracker-that-was-fun-for-me-an-adult-140043586.html)). The watch cost $229.95 plus a $9.99 monthly pass, and only parents see the data ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/fitbit/fitbit-ace-lte-smartwatch-kids/)).

Smaller entries fill in the edges. **Standland** wakes creatures when the player stands and puts them to sleep when the player sits, uses Stand Hours "when available; otherwise, Steps", and says it sends health data nowhere; reviewers say it goes stale once challenges are done [snippets] ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/standland/id1033409631), [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/standland/1033409631?country=us)). **Stompers**, by a three-person team, lets friends knock each other's step counts back in a daily race [snippet] ([developer newsletter](https://sorens.beehiiv.com/p/introducing-stompers)). **Monster Walk** launched in October 2025 to a 55% user score on one aggregator, disliked for stability [snippets] ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/monster-walk/official-launch-android-ios), [Game Brain](https://gamebrain.co/game/monster-walk-step-adventure)). **Garmin Jr.** unlocks an adventure step after 60 active minutes a day [snippet, retailer copy] ([JB Hi-Fi](https://www.jbhifi.com.au/products/garmin-vivofit-jr-3-fitness-trackers-stars-blue)). **Wokamon** and **Fitness RPG** convert steps to currency and are still listed [snippets] ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/wokamon-walking-app-game/id686364904)). **Run An Empire**, a GPS territory game, has had no update since July 2023 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/run-an-empire/id1073986257)). **Dragon Quest Walk** passed $2 billion by April 2024 [snippet] ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/dragon-quest-walk-revenue-exceeds-2-billion/)), but it is a Japan-only GPS gacha game and says little about a small calm one (inference).

Move-to-earn shows what happens when steps carry cash value. **Sweatcoin** says location data is essential and that the average user converts only about **65% of registered steps** ([Sweatcoin](https://sweatco.in/verification-algorithm)). **STEPN**'s anti-cheat flagged genuine users as bots in June 2022 [snippet] ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/stepn-launches-double-energy-event-to-apologise-for-flagging-users-as-bots/)), and its daily users fell from a reported 58,000 in June 2022 to 5,800 in September; other trackers give different absolute figures ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/111809/crypto-fitness-app-stepn-denies-layoff-reports)). A game whose steps are worth nothing outside it has no reason to distrust the player (inference).

### Sleep and recovery: one giant, a crowd of tiny pets, and one near-match

**Pokémon Sleep** is the largest product here: 30 million downloads by April 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Sleep)) and **$234.9 million lifetime spending** at its third anniversary, with year three at $68.2 million, down 3.2% (AppMagic estimates, [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokmon-sleep-surpasses-230m-in-three-years/)). It tracks sleep with the phone's microphone and accelerometer, and added smartwatches in September 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Sleep)), through Apple Health and Health Connect [snippet] ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/pokemon-sleep-now-supports-smartwatches-for-more-accurate-tracking-152113397.html)). Sleep is sorted into three types that decide which Pokémon appear, and a sleep score multiplied by Snorlax's strength decides how many ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Sleep)).

Its design statement is the most useful primary source in this report. The Pokémon Company wrote, "We intentionally did not design the app around rewarding specific sleep schedules", because that could push players to stay up or cut sleep short, and it dropped nightly videos of visiting Pokémon because watching them on waking might keep people in bed ([The Pokémon Company](https://corporate.pokemon.co.jp/en/topics/detail/110.html)). A forgotten night can be entered by hand; the entry restores helper energy but skips the Pokémon encounters [snippet] ([SeekingTech](https://seekingtech.com/how-to-manually-add-sleep-data-in-pokemon-sleep/)). Press reaction names the risk: a Digital Trends reviewer was "cautious of turning good sleep into something I feel I should be rewarded for" and objected to paid shortcuts ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/pokemon-sleep-one-week-later/)), and a TechRadar columnist reported worse nights from checking progress [snippet] ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/my-sleep-has-gotten-worse-since-playing-pokemon-sleep-but-i-still-wont-stop)). Practical complaints are battery drain and lost sessions [snippets] ([Dot Esports](https://vip-develop.dotesports.com/?p=914240)). What happens after several missed nights, and how late-arriving watch data is handled, were not found.

**SleepTown**, from the makers of Forest, is the clearest loss design: miss the bedtime and "you cannot construct a building", and using the phone during sleep time destroys the one under construction ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sleeptown/id1210251567)). One reviewer says that after missing bedtime they "immediately lose all motivation to actually go to sleep" [snippet] ([Apptail](https://apptail.io/app/sleeptown-mSxb/ireland)). Its last update was 23 November 2023. **Sleepagotchi** started with a rule to "reward only positive behaviour and not punish bad" ([MeinMMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/the-sleepagotchi-app-rewards-you-with-loot-boxes-when-you-go-to-bed-on-time-aims-to-improve-gamers-sleep,935597)), became a crypto gacha battler, and in May 2026 pivoted to an AI sleep coach [snippet] ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/sleepagotchi-pivots-from-sleep-to-an-ai-based-health-and-wellness-platform/)).

**Habitvs** is the nearest match to this project found anywhere. Its listing says it reads sleep, steps, activity rings, HRV, heart rate, resting heart rate and more from Apple Health; these "shape Kova's energy and mood", a slower day gives "a calmer response", quiet days remove no progress, health data "stays on your device", and its privacy label reads "Data Not Collected" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6762097932)). It differs in three ways: it shows a recovery score and alerts, it has a pet but no world, and it is iOS only. It is free, by a solo developer, with too few ratings to display. Its developer revised an "overreaching" alert to require a sustained signal over several days, which suggests single-night signals produce false alarms (inference).

The rest are tiny. **Slumber Drift** (March 2026) uses Apple Health sleep to move an airship, "fully offline", and **Daybound Tower** turns steps into tower floors ([developer site](https://www.tknzk.app/)). **Garmigotchi** is the anti-pattern: a Garmin watch-face pet that accumulates "neglect points" from low Body Battery, poor sleep and stress, and dies at 16 points until next month ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/jestersimpps/garmigotchi-a-tamagotchi-that-lives-off-your-health-data-1m6d)). A crowd of step-fed Apple Watch pets (StepDog, nomi, Tamawatchi, WatchieBesti, Hatchiko) rests on snippets only, and **Teddy**, a sleep pet, has 8 ratings and no update since August 2024 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/teddy-sleep-tracker-pet/id6526465688)). No indie sleep game on Health Connect, and nothing built directly on the Oura or Whoop APIs, was found in two searches, which is not proof of absence.

Recovery apps show the player what this project hides. Oura labels a readiness score under 70 "Pay attention", and its Rest Mode removes the activity goal and hides the activity score [snippets] ([Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/), [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/lv/articles/5949130374547-Glossary)). A March 2026 Harper's Bazaar column quotes psychologist Rachel Goldman, "When people check a readiness score before they've even checked in with themselves, it can shift everything", and sleep specialist Michael Breus, "It has made sleep into something you have to perform and score" ([AOL / Harper's Bazaar](https://www.aol.com/articles/oura-ring-judging-160543237.html)). Nintendo cancelled two body-sensing products over reliability; its Wii Vitality Sensor worked for only about 9 in 10 people [snippet] ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-07-05-the-wii-vitality-sensor-is-dead-at-least-for-now.html)).

### Late data and phone-only players are the normal case

The largest games treat late data as something to explain: both Scopely help pages promise delays of "up to several hours" and name battery-saver modes as a cause of missing steps ([Pikmin Bloom help](https://scopelyexplore.helpshift.com/hc/faq/2196-my-steps-aren-t-being-counted-correctly-ios/), [Pokémon GO help](https://scopelyexplore.helpshift.com/hc/faq/1325-troubleshooting-adventure-sync/)). The platforms make this structural. Apple states "the device encrypts the HealthKit store when the user locks the device. As a result, your app may not be able to read data from the store when it runs in the background" (verbatim per fetch, [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)), and caps step background delivery at hourly ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))). Developers report sleep data reaching the store many minutes or longer after waking (anecdotal, [Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825304)). On Android, background reads need an extra permission, and the default history window is 30 days before the first grant ([Health Connect](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/read-data)). Apple also hides denial: "an app isn't aware when the user denies permission to read data", so a game can only say there is no data yet ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)).

For players without a wearable, the evidence favours reading the health store over the phone sensor alone. Walkr's phone-only counting is its most-cited weakness, while on Android 14 with a recent system update Health Connect counts steps on the phone itself once any app holds the steps permission ([Health Connect](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/read-data)). Sleep without a wearable is harder: Pokémon Sleep needs the phone on the mattress, and its wearable import arrived 14 months after launch. Pokémon Sleep's manual entry, which keeps the day from being lost but holds the full reward for real data, is a working model for "not arrived yet" (inference).

### Retention fades in six to fourteen weeks, and nobody publishes game numbers

**No product in this research published retention curves**, and no post-mortem or conference talk from any of them was found; the nearest things are the 2017 Hon interview and The Pokémon Company's design post. What exists is research around them. A BMJ study of 560 Pokémon GO players found 955 extra steps a day in the first week and a return to baseline by week six ([Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2016-12-pokemon-moderately-physical-adults-effect.html)). A meta-analysis of 16 trials found gamification's effect on activity fell from g = 0.42 to g = 0.15 about 14 weeks after the intervention ended (figures from the abstract, [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779)). Vendor benchmarks put health and fitness apps at about 3 to 8% retention at day 30; Braze reports 3.9% on iOS ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention)). In the TRIPPA trial of 800 employees, a cash incentive raised steps at six months and left that group less active than the plain-tracker group at twelve ([TCTMD](https://www.tctmd.com/news/money-gadgets-incentivizing-healthy-behaviors-questioned-once-again)).

What separates the multi-year products from the fads, on this evidence, is inference: the long-lived ones (Pikmin Bloom, Pokémon Sleep, Walkr, Zombies, Run!) fit an existing routine, accumulate without decay, and keep adding content; the short-lived ones depended on novelty or an outside reward. The main long-term complaint about small collection games is running out of content (Walkr, Standland, Wokamon). Any retention target for this project is a guess.

### Products died from costs and platform changes, not from kindness

Stated reasons for shutdowns are commercial. Niantic laid off about 230 people in June 2023 and closed NBA All-World five months after launch, with its chief executive writing that the company had "allowed our expenses to grow faster than revenue" ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/pokemon-go-developer-confirms-230-layoffs-cancels-marvel-game-3463283)). Wizards Unite closed on 31 January 2022 with about $39.4 million lifetime revenue by third-party estimate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/02/harry-potter-wizards-unite-shut-down-niantic/)). Peridot, a creature game, left stores on 14 May 2026 with servers due to close on 31 August, Niantic citing the "enormous investment" of an always-on service ([iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/04/30/niantic-is-shutting-down-peridot/)). Fitbit removed Challenges and Adventures on 27 March 2023, deleting earned trophies, because of "a limited number of active users" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/03/27/fitbit-challenges-groups-removed/)).

Upstream APIs are the other risk. Google's migration FAQ says the Fit APIs will be deprecated in 2026 with no direct replacement for the REST API ([Google](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/migration/fit/faq)), and the legacy Fitbit Web API lists support ending 30 September 2026, with conflicting turn-off dates across Google's pages ([dev.fitbit.com](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/)). Even Health Connect changed under developers: from June 2026 on-device steps are attributed to a device-specific package name instead of "android" (verbatim per fetch, [Health Connect](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/read-data)). A game with no backend that reads only the two system health stores avoids most of these failure modes but still needs maintenance (inference).

### How the gentle ones stay motivating, and what it costs

Loss aversion works while it is applied. A loss-framed cash incentive was the only design that beat control in a 281-person trial, 45% versus 30% of days at goal, and the effect vanished when the incentive ended ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/2016-03-24/research/prospect-losing-money-pushes-people-achieve-fitness-goals), [Penn CHIBE](https://chibe.upenn.edu/blog/move-it-or-lose-it/)). Yet Duolingo's own published wins came from softening the loss: letting one lesson extend a streak raised day-14 retention by 3.3%, and allowing two streak freezes raised daily actives by 0.38% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak), [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)). **No controlled comparison of a no-loss design against a streak design was found.** The honest position is that this project's rules are defensible on wellbeing grounds and should be expected to cost some short-term retention (inference).

The costs of pressure are documented. Half of 141 lapsed Fitbit users felt guilty about stopping ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/09/160908141724.htm)). "Orthosomnia" was coined in a 2017 case series of patients who spent excessive time in bed to raise a tracker's number; it is not a recognised disorder ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/insomnia/orthosomnia)). In the best experiment found, 63 people with insomnia given false negative sleep feedback reported lower alertness and more fatigue [snippet] ([Oxford record](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publication/854408)), while one false score did nothing measurable to 54 healthy sleepers ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10899903/)). The harm concentrates in anxious sleepers, a likely audience for a calm sleep game (inference).

What replaces loss, in the products that avoid it, is a small set of devices. Gentler Streak counts rest days toward the streak and lets users declare themselves sick, injured or on holiday "to skip feeling guilty" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-fitness-tracker/id1576857102)); its designers aimed for "supportive but not cheesy, motivating but not fake-hyped" (verbatim per fetch, [Sketch](https://www.sketch.com/blog/gentler-streak/)). A user still wrote, "I don't like the 'reminders' about exercising or losing a streak" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-fitness-tracker/id1576857102)). Animal Crossing reduced its absence penalties with each release, until New Horizons kept only weeds, a few cockroaches and a remark from a villager ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/what-happens-when-you-abandon-your-animal-crossing-new-1844574997)); even then, an email telling lapsed players their island missed them drew criticism ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/nintendo-animal-crossing-new-horizons-hiatus-guilt-trip-email/)). Neko Atsume was built around "healing" and does not ask for attention ([Meta interview](https://www.meta.com/blog/neko-atsume-interview/)). Standland's idle state is sleep. Across these, the reasons to return are curiosity about what changed, attachment to a character, and accumulation that only rises; that list is a synthesis, not a cited finding.

### Google names games as an approved use; Apple wants Health use visible

All policy quotes below are verbatim per fetch and need re-checking on the live pages.

**Google Play** lists "Health-integrated games" among approved Health Connect use cases, on the conditions that "The primary purpose must remain gaming" and the app "should not function as an unregulated medical tool" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)). Every app must complete a Health apps declaration, and each Health Connect data type needs its own written justification ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/publish)). Stated rejection reasons include "Lack of clear justification for the requested permissions" and "Requesting a bulk list of permissions when not necessary" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)). Using the data for ads is prohibited ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)). The manifest must include an activity showing the same privacy policy as the store listing ([Health Connect](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/get-started)).

**Apple** has no games category for health data. Guideline 2.5.1 says "HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app", and 1.4.1 says unvalidated accuracy claims about health measurements lead to rejection ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The framework documentation requires that the use be "clear in both your marketing text and your user interface" and that a privacy policy exist ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)). Guideline 5.1.3(ii) forbids storing personal health information in iCloud. The design guidelines forbid ring-like elements for move, exercise and stand data, and say to call the store "Apple Health", never "HealthKit" ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit)).

The documented rejections are all Apple's. A step-count app was rejected in September 2025 because it "does not clearly identify the HealthKit and CareKit functionality in the app's user interface", and was rejected again after adding a prompt, an indicator and onboarding text; no resolution is recorded ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/802626)). Apps have been rejected under the same rule with no health calls at all, because a third-party framework linked HealthKit ([Apple forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/762128)). **No documented case of a game being approved or rejected specifically over health data was found on either store**; the shipping games above are the practical proof that approval is possible. A game that hides all health numbers sits in tension with 2.5.1, and plain "Connected to Apple Health" wording in onboarding, settings and the store description is the likely mitigation (inference, untested).

One Google risk is unresolved. Play requires an organization account, with a D-U-N-S number, for "Health apps, such as medical apps and human subjects research apps" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13634885)). Whether a game on a personal account that declares activity and sleep data is pushed onto an organization account is not settled by the rule text; one developer anecdote says it happened, but its source could not be opened.

On medical claims, the regulators' tests turn on intended purpose, meaning what the product claims. The US FDA reissued its general wellness guidance on 6 January 2026 with the two tests unchanged ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products)), and the EU guidance likewise turns on the manufacturer's claims ([OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)). Both were read only through law-firm and consultancy summaries.

### What nobody seems to be doing

Every line here is inference from the products found, and cannot establish absence.

- **A world, not a pet or a score, driven by several passive signals.** Studied products run on one signal, usually steps. Habitvs combines sleep, HRV and activity but has no world; Slumber Drift has a journey but uses sleep alone.
- **Recovery shaping the day's target inside a game.** It appears in Gentler Streak and Oura's Rest Mode, both trackers, and in no game found.
- **Progress with nothing to lose.** Even the gentle products keep a streak or a number: Gentler Streak, Habitvs's "grace weeks", and Finch, whose lost-streak complaints are noted below.
- **Hidden numbers.** Trackers compete on showing more; Habitvs shows its recovery score on tap.
- **Android.** Every sleep-driven indie found is iOS or Garmin.
- **On-device only, stated plainly.** Habitvs, Standland, nomi and Slumber Drift say so; none has visible traction.

Likely reasons the space is open (inference): no backend means no accounts or social features to monetise, no loss mechanic means giving up the best-measured retention lever, Apple's rule pulls health apps toward dashboards, and background limits make a passive world stale until opened.

## Part 2: A tall frame is filled by vertical structure, not by more sky

### No shipped game matches the brief; three partial matches cover it between them

A search of the App Store and Steam found **no game that is portrait, pixel art, a side-view lake and cabin, with day/night and weather**. Google Play, itch.io and developer blogs were not searched. The closest in subject, Cast n Chill, is a 16:9 desktop game; the closest in portrait composition, Odencat's story games, have no live sky; and the closest in time-of-day treatment, Mark Ferrari's Living Worlds, is a set of paintings with no character. Many well-known calm titles turned out to have landscape-only store screenshots, including Kingdom, Alto's Odyssey, Stardew Valley, Old Man's Journey and Tsuki's Odyssey ([Kingdom Two Crowns](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646), [Alto's Odyssey](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1182456409)); whether any rotates at runtime was not checked. Sword & Sworcery, often cited as a portrait pixel game, is explored in landscape and uses portrait only for combat and its book ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2011/03/23/superbrothers-sword-sworcery-ep-for-ipad-review/)). Among pixel artists, the formats checked were landscape or square for Kirokaze, Gutty Kreum and Slynyrd ([Inspiration Grid](https://theinspirationgrid.com/animated-pixel-art-creations-by-kirokaze), [SuperRare](https://superrare.com/editorial/7354)); Waneella sells a 2500x5000 vertical wallpaper, but the visible work is urban ([Gumroad](https://waneella.gumroad.com/l/WMWJUL21)).

### Art pixels and character size cluster in a narrow range

By the researchers' measurement, portrait pixel games on phones use **about 6 to 10 screen pixels per art pixel on a 1242-pixel-wide screenshot**, a canvas of roughly 130 to 210 art pixels wide. Odencat's three games all sit near 160x284. Living Worlds is finer, about 256 wide, because it shows paintings; Kingdom's 736 across is five times finer and depends on small detail spread across a wide strip. Characters in the portrait side-view examples take **8 to 15% of screen height**. No source states a rule for character size, and none of the viewed vertical artworks has a readable figure, so readability at the proposed proportions is untested.

The engine documentation does not solve portrait pixel art. Godot's pixel-art advice assumes viewports "between 256×224 and 640×480" with integer scaling, and its portrait-mobile advice is a separate case ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/multiple_resolutions.html)); a forum user noted "The documentation has a section on pixel art and a section on mobile, but not for pixel art on mobile" ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/what-are-the-right-resolution-aspect-mode-and-scaling-for-pixel-art-games-for-mobile/94804)). Flame's fixed-resolution camera "will underutilize the user's available screen space" on other aspect ratios, and its page says nothing about integer scaling ([Flame docs](https://docs.flame-engine.org/main/flame/camera.html)). The alternative, by a researcher's arithmetic and not from a source, is to fix the integer scale and let the visible height vary: at 180 art pixels wide and 6x on a 1080-pixel screen, the view runs from 320 rows at 9:16 to 420 rows at 9:21, so about 100 rows of sky and water must be optional. No source on notches or safe areas for pixel art was found.

### Depth comes from colour steps, overlap and a ground seen from above

The written method comes mostly from Slynyrd's Pixelblog. "The closest plane is the most saturated with strongest contrast between light and shadow", and each plane back loses saturation, gains lightness and moves toward the sky colour; near grass shows blades and far planes use one- or two-pixel clusters ([Pixelblog 62](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2026/5/27/pixelblog-62-landscape-backgrounds)). Height in the frame is itself a cue: "The closer an object is to the vertical position of the horizon, the more distant it appears" ([Pixelblog 11](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/11/16/pixelblog-11-landscape-pixeling)). The mirror pond applies this with three value steps (near-black grass, mid-dark pines, light mountains) and a dark U-shaped foreground frame (researcher's reading of the image).

Parallax is a weak tool for a scene that barely scrolls. Slynyrd writes that it "may not be convincing if your scene doesn't already convey depth", that movement must be in whole art pixels, and that speeds under one pixel per frame "can look a bit jittery" ([Pixelblog 23](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/11/12/pixelblog-23-parallax-scrolling)). Foreground objects passing in front of the scene do appear: Cast n Chill puts a large dark tree over the top-right quarter and dark grass in a bottom corner, and Bear's Restaurant's night scene has dark bushes across the bottom 8% (researcher's estimates).

**No written source explains the convention of a ground strip seen slightly from above in a side-view scene.** The evidence is visual: Ferrari's pond and snow path are seen from above while trees and mountains stay in side elevation, and Bear's Restaurant fills its lower half with a tilted grass plane instead of a flat line.

### Interface: a bare scene is workable, and slabs over 18% change the reading

Bear's Restaurant shows a full-screen scene with no interface beyond one line of text at about 75% of the height, and Living Worlds uses translucent buttons along the top and a thin strip at the bottom. Where a bottom bar exists it is commonly 8 to 10% of the height (Fishing Paradiso, [Pixel Reel](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6790169029), A Girl Adrift). Slabs of 18 to 22% ([Disco Zoo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id794524208), [Hungry Hearts Diner](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1255042213)) already read as an interface with a scene above (inference). Overlay text sits most comfortably on a dark or flat band, such as Fishing Paradiso's cave ceiling. Phone wallpaper artists leave the top third calm for the lock-screen clock ([franrekk](https://franrekk.gumroad.com/l/etdko)); three meters have the same need (inference).

### Time of day and weather are layers over shared art in every example

No example redraws the scene per condition. Three methods have sources.

**Palette substitution.** Ferrari's originals are single 640x480 images with one 256-colour palette ([Effect Games](http://www.effectgames.com/effect/article-Old_School_Color_Cycling_with_HTML5.html)), and the demo's scene list offers the same drawings as morning, night, rain, snow and fog variants ([scene list](http://www.effectgames.com/demos/canvascycle/scenes.js)). In the app, "The sun rises and falls in real time, storms pass through" ([Pixfabrik](http://pixfabrik.com/livingworlds/)). This requires indexed-colour art (inference).

**Lookup tables.** Graveyard Keeper's artist "created 10 different LUTs for different time of day"; its fog is many horizontal sprites thinned toward the top so roofs and treetops stay clear, and its wind is a shader that moves the highest points most ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/graveyard-keeper-how-the-graphics-effects-are-made)). How the tables blend is not stated.

**Tint plus lights.** Usagi Shima, a cozy game on a real clock, interpolates a whole-canvas tint every minute, for example from sunset `Color(0.99, 0.85, 0.75)` to night `Color(0.39, 0.51, 0.64)`, and fades in additive lights on windows and lanterns ([Usagi Shima devlog](https://usagishima.net/2021/04/13/devlog-2-real-time-day-night-cycle/)).

In screenshots, Kingdom shows night, fog and rain as whole-scene colour changes plus particles, and Cast n Chill shows mist, autumn, snow and a moonlit night over one layer structure (researcher's reading). For water, Slynyrd's recipe is "Draw scene, reflect layer, add highlights, apply effect" ([Pixelblog 10](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/10/12/pixelblog-10-water-in-motion)); a flipped reflection inherits the sky's time-of-day colour for free (inference). For ambient motion, Yuuta Toyoi mixes loop lengths, "some looping at multiples of 3 and some looping at multiples of 4", so the repeat goes unnoticed ([Pixivision](https://www.pixivision.net/en/a/2686)).

### What works, what wastes the frame, and who to study

Approaches that fill a tall frame, each tied to a viewed example:

- **Mirrored water as the lower third to half.** The mirror pond gives 35 to 40% of its height to water, so the mountains appear twice and sky stays at 10 to 15%.
- **A tall near object.** The conifer in Bear's Restaurant, the full-height trunks in Ferrari's winter forest, the trunk in Timberman.
- **A ground or water plane seen from above**, stacking distance up the frame.
- **A view below the waterline**, as in Cast n Chill's night shot (underwater from 63% down) and Pixel Reel.
- **A character at the bottom, seen from behind, looking out**, as in Fishing Paradiso.
- **A structured sky**, such as a cumulus bank, in place of a flat gradient.

Approaches that waste it:

- **Letterboxing a landscape strip.** Bear's Restaurant's interiors and most of [Mousebusters](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502065641) use about half the height.
- **A scene card above an interface slab.** [You Must Build A Boat](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id811397653) gives its scene 10 to 15%; [Postknight 2](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1497250814) about 35 to 40%.
- **The Kingdom recipe unchanged.** A thin ground strip between large sky and reflection bands becomes a stripe between two flat areas (inference).
- **A fixed logical resolution**, which leaves bars on phones of other shapes.

A researcher's proposed split, extrapolated mainly from the mirror pond and not taken from any source: calm sky 15 to 25%, far hills and tree line 20 to 25%, shoreline with cabin, character and dog 15 to 20%, mirrored water 25 to 35%, dark foreground fringe 5 to 10%. Sky and water are the bands to stretch on taller phones.

Worth studying: **Odencat** for portrait scenes at phone scale, **Wombat Brawler** (Cast n Chill, with a [demo](https://wombatbrawler.itch.io/cast-n-chill-demo)) for the subject and its layering, **Mark Ferrari** for time of day by palette, **Slynyrd** for written method, and **Raw Fury's Kingdom** for reflection and grading. [Cornerpond](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3454590), a small upright desktop fishing window, deserves a closer look than it got.

## Conclusion

The research moves two assumptions. First, the risk to this game is less "will a gentle design hold people" than "will the player understand why the world looks the way it does this morning". The loudest anger in the field is about unexplained undercounting, and the store rule most likely to bite, Apple's 2.5.1, also asks for the health link to be visible. One plain sentence per visible change, already a project rule, answers both, and a clear "last night has not arrived yet" state is a feature the biggest games only offer in a help article. Second, the portrait problem is a composition problem before it is a rendering one: the frame fills when the lake is the lower third and mirrors the hills, a near tree runs up one side, and the shoreline is seen from slightly above, and none of that depends on parallax.

Three things remain open and cheap to settle. Ask Play Console support about the organization-account question before Phase 1. Build a portrait mock at about 160 to 180 art pixels wide to test whether a character at 8 to 15% of the height reads beside a cabin and a dog. And plan a local backup path now, since lost progress on a new phone is the worst review theme of the longest-lived indie in this set, and an on-device game inherits that risk in full.
