# Pixel-art games and apps that show a side-view landscape in a tall portrait frame

State as of November 2026.

How to read these notes:

- **"Inspected"** means I downloaded the store screenshots and looked at them myself. The source link is the store page that hosts those screenshots.
- **Band percentages are my estimates**, read off a 10% grid laid over the screenshot (0% = top of screen, 100% = bottom). Expect an error of about 3 points.
- **Art pixel size is my measurement**, not a developer statement: I measured the length of same-colour pixel runs in the full-resolution store screenshot (1242x2208 for portrait iPhone shots) and divided. "Logical resolution" is the screenshot size divided by that number. I found no developer statement of logical resolution for any game here.
- Store screenshots are chosen by the publisher and some are marketing composites, so they may not show every screen of the game.
- Nothing here comes from memory alone. Where I could not check something, it is in Gaps.

## Which portrait pixel-art games show a side-view (or near side-view) scene, and how do they divide the tall frame?

### Takeaway
I found no shipped game that combines portrait orientation, pixel art, a side-view lake and cabin, and day/night plus weather; the closest match in subject (Cast n Chill) is desktop 16:9, and the closest in portrait composition (Bear's Restaurant, Fishing Paradiso) are story games without a live sky. Portrait pixel games that do fill the frame use one of four devices: a tall foreground object, a tilted ground plane filling the lower half, a view below the waterline, or vertical stacking.

### Inferences
- Portrait pixel games on phones cluster at 6–10 screen pixels per art pixel on a 1242-wide screenshot, a logical canvas of roughly 130–210 art pixels wide by 230–370 tall. Odencat's three games all sit at about 160x284. Living Worlds is finer (about 256 wide) because it shows paintings rather than a playable character.
- Character height in the portrait side-view examples is 8–15% of screen height (Fishing Paradiso 8%, Bear's Restaurant 13% seated, A Girl Adrift 13%, Timberman 15%). In Cast n Chill's 16:9 frame the angler is 5–10% of frame height; scaled into portrait at the same art pixel size he would be a much smaller share of the height.
- A scene with no HUD is workable in portrait: Bear's Restaurant and Living Worlds both show a full-screen scene with at most one text line or a thin translucent strip.
- For a lake-and-cabin scene, the Cast n Chill cabin shot rotated into portrait proportions suggests a band order: sky, far mountains, tree line with the cabin, shore, dock with character and dog, then water. The water band at the bottom is the one that can stretch on taller phones.

### Gaps
- No shipped game found that matches portrait + pixel art + side-view lake and cabin + day/night + weather. This is from App Store and Steam searches only; I did not search Google Play, itch.io, Reddit or developer blogs.
- No developer statements on logical resolution or scaling for any game above; all pixel sizes are my measurements from store screenshots, and those marked uncertain had inconsistent run lengths.
- Cast n Chill: I found no evidence of an iOS or Android version or a portrait mode. I did not measure its art pixel size.
- How these games handle narrow or extra-tall phones (cropping the sides, extending the sky or water, letterboxing) could not be determined from store screenshots, which are all one aspect ratio.
- Downwell's store image is an upscaled JPEG; pixel size not measurable.
- Not found in App Store search, so not checked: Luna's Fishing Garden, Desert Island Fishing, Virtual Cottage, Chill Corner, Abyssrium Pole, Kiki's Vacation, a mobile Summerhouse.

## Which well-known references are landscape-only, and what do they still teach?

### Takeaway
Most of the famous calm side-view pixel landscapes are landscape-only: Kingdom on phones, and the desktop idle games Cast n Chill, Cornerpond and Rusty's Retirement. They are still the best source for layering, reflection and weather treatment, but their band recipe cannot be copied into a tall frame unchanged.

### Cited Findings
- Kingdom Two Crowns on the App Store has landscape screenshots only — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)
- Kingdom Two Crowns band split in a 16:9 frame (my estimate): sky and cloud bank 0–35%; trees and buildings 17–57%; a thin ground strip 57–63%; water reflection 63–100% with short ripple dashes. No HUD is visible — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)
- Kingdom Two Crowns screenshots show night, a red moon, fog and rain done as whole-scene colour changes plus particles — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)
- Kingdom Two Crowns art pixel size (my measurement): about 3 screen pixels per art pixel on 2208x1242, about 736x414 logical; the reflection uses single-pixel rows — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)
- Kingdom: New Lands also has landscape screenshots only — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1118513354)
- Cornerpond (foolsroom, released April 21, 2025, Windows) is a small window in the corner of the desktop: a character on a pier, water below, and UI buttons in a strip under the scene — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3454590)
- Rusty's Retirement (Mister Morris Games, released April 26, 2024) is a wide strip along the bottom of the desktop, the opposite extreme of format — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2666510)
- Other calm titles I checked that have landscape-only store screenshots: The Longing Mobile — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1664728844); Alto's Odyssey — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1182456409); Rain City — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1371241818); Under Leaves — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1082189848); Old Man's Journey — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1204902987); Stardew Valley — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1406710800); Campfire Cat Cafe — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6443801418)
- Fishing and Life has landscape screenshots: flat vector side view with a cabin on stilts at the left, a sun, layered hills and UI in the corners — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1457176367)
- Tsuki's Odyssey has landscape screenshots and a three-quarter top-down view, not a side view — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1564146071)

### Inferences
- Kingdom's recipe (sky about 35%, scene about 25%, ground strip about 6%, reflection about 37%) works because the frame is wide. Kept at the same proportions in portrait, the scene band would be a thin stripe between two large flat areas. A portrait version needs taller content in the scene band (tall trees, a mountain) or structure inside the sky and water bands.
- Kingdom's art is far finer than phone portrait games (about 736 logical pixels across versus about 160). A portrait game at Odencat's scale has roughly a fifth of the horizontal art pixels, so it cannot rely on small detail spread across a wide strip.
- Cornerpond is the nearest desktop format to a phone screen (a small upright window with the scene above and buttons below) and is worth a closer look than its fame suggests.

### Gaps
- I did not play any of these; whether Kingdom or the others rotate to portrait at runtime is not confirmed, only that their store screenshots are landscape.
- Cornerpond and Rusty's Retirement were inspected only as small contact-sheet images; no band estimates or pixel measurements.

## What fills the tall frame well, and what wastes it?

### Takeaway
The frame is filled by vertical structure inside the scene (a tall tree, a tilted ground plane, a view below the waterline, a vista seen from behind the character), and wasted when a landscape strip is letterboxed, pushed into a card above an interface slab, or left floating in a flat gradient.

### Cited Findings

#### Devices that fill the height
- One tall foreground object spanning much of the height: the conifer in Bear's Restaurant (crown 0–40% of height) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699); the full-height trunk in Timberman — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id871809581); framing trunks and a waterfall in Living Worlds — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1371140984)
- A tilted ground plane instead of a flat side-view line, so ground fills the lower half: Bear's Restaurant grass at 48–100% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699)
- A cross-section below the waterline: Cast n Chill night shot, underwater at 63–100% — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3483740); Pixel Reel, water with fish at about 45–92% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6790169029); Ridiculous Fishing EX, scrolling down through depth — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1610164691)
- Characters seen from behind at the bottom, looking out over a vista that rises up the screen: Fishing Paradiso cave — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1441716978); Mousebusters rooftop — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502065641)
- Vertical stacking of side-view rooms: Tiny Tower — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiny-tower-tap-idle-evolution/id422667065); Idle Apocalypse — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1394442481)
- Dark foreground framing at the edges and corners (branches above, grass below) that gives the sky and water bands something to hold: Cast n Chill — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3483740); dark bushes in the bottom 8% of the Bear's Restaurant night scene — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699)
- A large structured sky (a cumulus bank taking about 40%) rather than a flat gradient: Living Worlds — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1371140984); Kingdom Two Crowns cloud bank — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)

#### Patterns that waste the height
- Letterboxing a landscape strip with dark bars: interiors in Bear's Restaurant — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699); most scenes in Mousebusters, about 50% of the height used — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502065641)
- Scene strip plus interface slab: You Must Build A Boat, scene about 10–15% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id811397653); Postknight 2, side-view scene in a top card of about 35–40% with UI below — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1497250814); Disco Zoo, bottom slab about 22% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id794524208); Hungry Hearts Diner, bottom navigation about 18% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1255042213)
- An object floating in a flat gradient with large empty areas: Age of 2048 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1234123014)
- Dense HUD on every edge of the scene: Cats&Soup — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1581431235); Idle Apocalypse, with button columns on both sides and a bottom panel of about 21% — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1394442481)

#### Time of day and weather treatments seen
- Palette swap over the same painting, with a clock time shown: Living Worlds — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1371140984)
- Whole-scene colour grading plus particles for night, fog and rain: Kingdom Two Crowns — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1477991646)
- Sky tint carried through the whole scene (dusk orange, red, night purple): A Girl Adrift — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1190528271)

- Palette swaps for autumn and a rainy night with rain particles, in a non-pixel portrait game: Cats&Soup — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1581431235)
- A lamp light cone as an overlay in a night scene: Bear's Restaurant — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699)
- Backdrop and palette swaps as "themes" over unchanged gameplay art: Timberman — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id871809581)

#### Non-pixel portrait scenes worth a glance for composition
- Focus Traveller: illustrated portrait forest and snow scenes with tall trees as framing and small hikers in the bottom third — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1559730367)
- Fantasy Hike: silhouette side-view landscape on a dusk gradient in the upper half, text below — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1557127861)

### Inferences
- The two most transferable devices for a lake-and-cabin scene are a tall near tree (as in Bear's Restaurant and Cast n Chill) and a deep water band with reflection or a look below the surface. Together they give vertical structure to the top and bottom of the frame, where a flat side view has the least to show.
- A strict side view with a thin ground line (the Kingdom recipe) is the weakest fit for portrait. Every portrait example that works either tilts the ground, stacks vertically, or makes one band (water, trunk, sky) the subject.
- Overlay text sits most comfortably on a dark or flat band: the cave ceiling in Fishing Paradiso, the lower grass in Bear's Restaurant. Three meters could sit on such a band without a panel.
- The weather and time-of-day treatments in every example are grading, palette or particle layers over shared art; none redraws the scene per condition. That matches a layered build (sky, lighting and grade, particles, foliage).
- If only one bottom bar is needed, 8–10% of the height is the common size (Fishing Paradiso, Pixel Reel, A Girl Adrift); slabs of 18–22% already read as "interface with a scene above".

### Gaps
- All of this is from still screenshots. I did not see motion: parallax when scrolling, how the camera follows a character sideways in portrait, or how weather transitions animate.
- No source found that discusses portrait composition for pixel landscapes directly (no devlogs, talks or articles were fetched). The "what works" list is my reading of the screenshots.
- I found no pixel-art example of live local weather driving a full landscape scene; Pixel Weather shows weather as a card with an icon and a tiny ground line, not a landscape.

## Which studios and artists are worth studying?

### Takeaway
Odencat is the best single reference for portrait pixel scenes at phone scale; Wombat Brawler (Cast n Chill) is the best reference for the subject and the layering; Mark Ferrari's Living Worlds is the reference for time of day by palette.

### Cited Findings
- Odencat ships several portrait pixel story games at one consistent scale (about 160x284 logical by my measurement): Bear's Restaurant — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1425197699); Fishing Paradiso — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1441716978); Mousebusters — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502065641); Meg's Monster (listing found, screenshots not inspected) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1550415965)
- Wombat Brawler: Cast n Chill, an angler and dog on lakes with a cabin, seasons and day/night; a demo is on itch.io — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3483740); [itch.io](https://wombatbrawler.itch.io/cast-n-chill-demo)
- Mark Ferrari / Pixfabrik: Living Worlds, landscape pixel paintings with palette-driven time of day, presented full-screen in portrait — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1371140984)

### Inferences
- Study order for this project: Bear's Restaurant first (portrait scene with no HUD at the likely pixel scale), then Cast n Chill (how to layer a lake, cabin, dog and weather), then Living Worlds (time of day by palette), then Kingdom (reflection and grading).
- Playing the Cast n Chill demo and capturing a portrait-shaped crop of its scenes would be the cheapest test of how its layering survives a tall frame.
